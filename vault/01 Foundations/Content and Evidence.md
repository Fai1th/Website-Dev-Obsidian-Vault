# Content and Evidence

Before layout, write the user task and what the visitor must believe or know to complete it. A section without a clear purpose is a candidate for removal.

Use specific verbs, concrete service names and accurate product descriptions. Avoid stock claims such as seamless, next-generation or revolutionary when they carry no information. Label prototypes and sample data clearly.

Do not invent endorsements, logos, awards, user counts or precise specifications. Real testimonials retain meaning, attribution and permission. Do not rewrite a quote as if the speaker said the edited wording; use an approved excerpt or clearly marked paraphrase.

Use one action label per intent across nav, body and footer. Repeating the same action at useful decision points is allowed. Distinguish primary, secondary and destructive actions by behavior, not colour alone.

For professional services, state scope, audience, contact method and response expectations when known. For commerce, show material information before commitment. For applications, label what saving, deleting and sharing actually do.

Check every visible string, alt text, error, title and footer destination. No dead CTAs, fake scarcity, decorative technical metadata or unreviewed generated copy at release.
